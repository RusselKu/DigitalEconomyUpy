What do I observe?

The Netherlands (92.14) and New Zealand (55.77) dominate in server density and R&D. Argentina (55.26) leads the Latin American cohort in digital service exports (64.2%).

What does it mean?

Mexico possesses solid consumer adoption, but creates minimal intangible intellectual property, creating a dependency on foreign AgTech algorithms.

What can I NOT conclude?

A low national score does not mean Mexican commercial farming is unproductive; it indicates that agricultural technology is imported rather than locally developed.

Q1

Mexico's Relative Standing

Mexico ranks 4th in the group with a Digital Readiness Score of 26.13 points, lagging behind the Netherlands (92.14), New Zealand (55.77), and Argentina (55.26), outperforming only Kenya (15.42).

Q2

Primary Competitive Strength

Robust internet user penetration (81.2%) and an affordable entry-level broadband basket (1.95% of GNI per capita), supported by established electronics hardware manufacturing.

Q3

Primary Structural Gap

A severe deficit in backend infrastructure, domestic R&D, and intellectual property: recording only 412 secure servers per million people, 0.27% of GDP in R&D, and 8 resident patents per million, preventing the creation of proprietary AgTech software.

Q4

Most Compelling Benchmark

Argentina, which as an upper-middle-income Latin American peer exports 64.2% of its services in digitally deliverable format and 15.9% in ICT services, proving Mexico can transition to high-margin knowledge services without waiting for European-level GDP per capita.

Q5

Core Dataset Limitation

Country-level aggregation masks stark internal regional divides (technified northern agribusiness vs. rural southern smallholders) and lacks direct metrics on IoT-equipped agricultural acreage or dedicated AI GPU infrastructure.
